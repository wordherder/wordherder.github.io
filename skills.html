Ontology & Knowledge Engineering

The hardest part of building an ontology is deciding what not to model.

I am currently building a formal OWL ontology at NBCUniversal that gives a language model a bounded world to classify against: nine nodes covering activities, affects, characters, countries, interactions, objects, story elements, travel, and video, with mostly transitive relationships among them. The temptation on a project like this is to model the whole world. I didn't. The goal was to describe only the world that exists inside a particular set of reality television shows, which is a far smaller and far more useful thing to own. I work in Protégé and TopQuadrant, write in RDF, OWL, and SKOS, and query with SPARQL. I also spent five years at Yellowbook writing the requirements, test cases, and user manual for an in-house ontology management system, so I have seen this work from the maintenance side as well as the modeling side.

Taxonomy & Controlled Vocabulary

A controlled vocabulary is only as good as the language real people actually use.

Every node in the NBCUniversal ontology has a taxonomy underneath it, because an ontology tells a model how things relate and a taxonomy tells it what to call them. I build those vocabularies from evidence rather than from a whiteboard. When I compared search behavior across four Vanguard properties, I used the topic set derived from the largest dataset as the controlled vocabulary for all four rather than inventing one. Before that I managed the subject headings and keyword taxonomy at Yellowbook, and documented the search requirements for STIBO Step at Ferguson. The two ways I have watched a taxonomy fail are over-granularity and aspiration: terms so fine that nobody can choose between them, and terms that describe the content someone hopes to have rather than the content that exists.

Search Relevance & Findability

Ten years on a single question: why didn't they find the thing that was there the whole time?

At Vanguard I replaced Autonomy with Coveo on the Personal Investor search page and took no-results searches from nearly 12% down to 0.68%, raised search usage from under 1% of site traffic to 5%, and cut negative survey feedback by 87%. At Ferguson I reduced search-to-product clicks three to one and dropped no-results searches by 78%. At TE Connectivity I put part numbers into the autosuggest, which collapsed the shortest path to a buyable product from three clicks to one. The part that transfers between jobs is not the platform but the diagnosis: working out whether a bad search is the engine's fault, the content's fault, or a mismatch between how the index was built and how the customer thinks.

Query Analysis & Data

Query logs are the largest body of unprompted user testimony a company owns, and almost nobody reads them.

For the Vanguard cross-site study I hand-tagged 42,765 queries across four properties, 59% of everything submitted in the window, because the organization held a widely shared belief that its search complaints came from customers landing on the wrong site. The data said otherwise: nearly 80% of the queries submitted on Corporate would have returned better results on the other three properties. The customer was not lost. The content was. At TE I ran regular expressions across the top million queries in a 90-day window to find customers typing Boolean operators into a single search box, which turned out to be under 5% of users but a consistent and identifiable group worth building for. I work in SQL, SPARQL, Python, regular expressions, R, DBT, and Athena, with Adobe and Google Analytics for the behavioral side.

LLM Evaluation & Prompt Engineering

A model that sounds right and a model that is right are different things, and only one of them is measurable.

The current generation of large language models are token predictors trained on an enormous quantity of data, not reasoning engines, and treating them accordingly gets better results than treating them as oracles. My ontology work at NBCUniversal is scored on precision and recall against the model's classification of video clips. The first round came back with decent precision and recall that was still far too high, so the recommendation was to tighten the taxonomies rather than to keep rewriting the prompt. I engineer the prompts the model uses to assemble clip groups for clipblock definitions, and I use models to write the Python and VBA that handles my own repetitive work. I completed the Agentic AI bootcamp at Data Science Dojo in 2025.
